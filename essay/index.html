How I Learned Computational Neuroscience

I came to computational neuroscience through biology. These are the courses, books, and research experiences that helped me learn its mathematical and computational foundations.

By Safaa Mamoun · 5 min read

Learning is more accessible than ever

Courses, books, tutorials, and AI tutors have made specialized knowledge available to anyone willing to learn. During my PhD, I wanted to understand not only whether an animal moved more or less, but how behaviour was organized over time, how states transitioned, and how those dynamics changed with age or social isolation. That goal shaped the learning path below.

What I would tell another biologist

You do not need to become a theoretical physicist before you are allowed to ask computational questions. You need enough mathematics to understand your model, enough programming to examine your data honestly, and enough neuroscience to know when an elegant result is biologically meaningless.

My route was not linear. I repeatedly returned to fundamentals, learned a technique when a real problem demanded it, and then revisited the theory with better questions. That loop — foundation, application, confusion, return — is still how I learn.
